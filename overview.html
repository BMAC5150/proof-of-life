DOC. Overview
CLASS. Advance Review Copy

For four and a half hours one Tuesday morning in February, sixty-seven thousand people in New York did not exist.

Their banks did not know them. Their pharmacies did not know them. The banks called it an inconvenience.

The cause was a bad code update from Sovren, a four-person fintech startup in a Chinatown walkup whose software cleans up duplicate customer records for community banks. Then one of the startup’s investors sent Sovren’s confidential technical documentation to Tehran.

With American carriers closing on Iran’s coast, young Iranian engineers in a bunker outside Karaj race to convert Sovren’s software into the one weapon their country can aim at an American city before the war starts. It is not a bomb. It is the power to erase the people who hold a city together.

To prove it works, they erase nearly twenty thousand people in a college town in upstate New York in fourteen minutes. Then the demands arrive. Forty-eight hours, or over two million people from Cleveland to Boston follow.

In the Chinatown walkup, the engineer who wrote Sovren’s code works through the night with a lone analyst at Cyber Command to take the Iranian weapon apart. Every lock they break has another under it. The man who built those locks is in Karaj, and he knew they would come.

In Washington, the people who could stop it are deciding whether they would rather keep it.

For readers of Tom Clancy and John le Carré.
